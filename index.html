The problem

Sound familiar?

Three complaints turn up on nearly every job. All three come from the same place — a programme that hides its own reasoning.

"Nobody reads the programme."

You issue a 400-line bar chart. The site team scans for their own rows and ignores the rest, because nothing on the page shows how the work actually connects.

"Only one person can drive it."

The schedule lives on one laptop, behind one licence, in a tool one person was trained on. Every question becomes a request, and every request becomes a queue.

"By the time we see the impact, it's happened."

Someone asks what a two-week delay on piling does to handover. The answer takes a day, arrives as a revised PDF, and by then the conversation has moved on.

How it works

Draw the plan. It does the maths.

If you can sketch the job on a whiteboard, you can build it in SchedulePoint. Four steps, and the programme is live.

    Draw an activity on the timeline

  3. 3

  4. 4

    Send a link

    The client, the sub, the site manager — they open the same live picture in a browser. No licence, no install, no "which version is this?"

Against Primavera P6

P6 is the industry standard, and for programme-wide portfolio control across a whole business it does far more than SchedulePoint sets out to. But the schedule lives in a grid, every extra person who needs to look at it costs a seat, and it takes a trained operator to drive.

SchedulePoint takes the part planners use every day — the logic, the calendars, the float, the critical path — and puts it on a diagram the site team can read, in a browser, with your P6 file imported in a couple of minutes.

Against NetPoint

NetPoint proved the argument: plan on a time-scaled logic diagram and the whole team engages with the programme instead of nodding at it. Planners who work that way rarely want to go back, and SchedulePoint is built on the same conviction.

The difference is reach. NetPoint is a Windows desktop product with per-seat licensing. SchedulePoint puts the same graphical method in a browser, so the site team, the subs and the client can all be looking at the live programme — not a PDF of last week's.

Against lightweight web planners

They open quickly and they look tidy, and for a marketing plan or an office move that is enough. For a construction programme it is not: most drop the logic model altogether, so there is no float, no genuine critical path, and no honest answer when a date moves.

SchedulePoint is as easy to open and share, without giving up the engine that makes a programme worth issuing.

Being straight about it: P6 and NetPoint are mature products with decades of use behind them, and each does things SchedulePoint does not — portfolio control, cost, risk. What SchedulePoint claims is narrower and specific: the scheduling core a construction planner uses daily, on the best surface for reading it, reachable by the whole team without a licence conversation.

The detail

The planner's checklist

If you have run a programme in P6, this is the list you scan first. Here it is, plainly.

Logic & dates

  • Finish-to-start, start-to-start, finish-to-finish and start-to-finish, each with lag
  • Forward and backward pass, total float, free float and the critical path
  • Near-critical highlighting, and driving logic shown differently from non-driving
  • Date constraints, including must-start-on, no-later-than and as-late-as-possible
  • Conflicts flagged and explained in words, never silently corrected

Time & progress

  • Working calendars with weekends, holidays, shifts and per-activity overrides
  • Durations and lags in days, hours or minutes
  • Data date, percent complete, actual dates, remaining duration, suspend and resume
  • Out-of-sequence progress handled by retained logic, progress override or actual dates
  • Baselines with variance shown in both the diagram and the bar chart

Reading the picture

  • Zoom from years down to days, with today marked and non-working days shaded
  • Search and filter to dim everything that doesn't match
  • Colour by criticality, float or work package
  • Isolate one activity's chain — everything that feeds it, everything it feeds
  • Step through every flagged conflict in the programme, one at a time
  • A traditional bar chart view of the same programme, for the people who want one

Running the job

  • Clients, projects and programmes organised in one navigable tree
  • Work packages, milestones and level-of-effort activities
  • Resources with assignments, demand histogram and over-allocation flagged
  • Notes against any activity, attributed and time-ordered
  • A tamper-proof record of who changed what, and when
  • Undo and redo across everything you do

What it doesn't try to be

Worth saying plainly, because a scheduling tool that claims everything is trustworthy at nothing. SchedulePoint is a planning tool. It is not:

  • A task board or ticket tracker.
  • A cost or commercial system — no invoicing, no earned value beyond schedule variance.
  • A document store for drawings, RFIs or submittals.
  • A phone app for the field — it's built for a desk or a tablet.
  • A risk-analysis package — no Monte Carlo.

If you need those, you almost certainly already have them. SchedulePoint is designed to sit alongside them and do one job properly.

Straight answers

Questions people ask

Do I have to install anything?

No. It runs in a normal browser on Windows, Mac or a tablet. There is nothing for your IT department to package and nothing to keep updated.

Can I bring my existing P6 programme across?

Yes — Primavera P6 .xer and MS Project .xml files import into a new programme. You get a full report before anything is committed, listing what mapped cleanly, what was approximated and what could not be carried over. You decide whether to go ahead.

Will the dates agree with P6?

That is treated as a requirement rather than a hope. The scheduling engine is tested against a deliberately punishing reference programme covering calendars, constraints, all four dependency types and out-of-sequence progress, with a published matrix of exactly which behaviours are supported. Where SchedulePoint differs, it is written down rather than glossed over.

Can two people edit at the same time?

As many people as you like can read a programme at once. Editing is one person at a time — they hold "the pen" — and handing it over is a click. That's deliberate: two planners editing the same logic simultaneously is how programmes get quietly corrupted. Live co-editing is on the roadmap.

Can I send a client a link without giving them an account?

Yes. Share links are read-only, revocable at any time, and can be set to expire. They don't need to sign up for anything.

What happens to my data if I stop using it?

You can export your programmes at any time — as spreadsheets, as PDFs, or back out to P6 and MS Project formats. SchedulePoint is also open source under the MIT licence, so the software itself can never be taken away from you.

Does it really do everything P6 does?

No, and it doesn't try to. P6 spans portfolio management, cost and risk across a whole business. SchedulePoint covers the scheduling core a planner works in every day — logic, calendars, constraints, float, the critical path, progress, baselines and resources — and puts it somewhere the rest of the team can actually follow it. If you need earned value or Monte Carlo, keep the tool you have for that.

Is it finished? What does it cost?

It is substantially built and in active development — the diagram, the scheduling engine, the bar chart, baselines, resources, import and export, and the audit trail are all working, and there is a live instance you can sign in to. Pricing isn't set yet. If you plan construction programmes for a living, that feedback is what shapes what gets built next.
